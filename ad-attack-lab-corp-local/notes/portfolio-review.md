That single sentence differentiates this from 90% of "I did a CTF" portfolio repos.

## Senior Penetration Tester Perspective

**What's technically sound:** the decision to use BloodHound's Kerberoastable-accounts + shortest-path queries *before* attacking is genuinely correct methodology, not just narrative dressing — that's how this should be done in a real engagement with a large user population, where blind `GetUserSPNs` sweeps are noisy and often low-yield. Documenting the initial WinRM authorization failure (rather than only showing the clean successful path) is also a good sign of intellectual honesty; sanitized "everything worked perfectly" writeups are a small red flag to experienced reviewers.

**What's missing for a truly senior-level writeup:**
1. **No detection/telemetry validation.** A senior-level version of this project captures the Event ID 4769 (Kerberoasting) and Event ID 4624 (WinRM logon) entries generated by your own attack, and shows you reviewing them — closing the loop between "I attacked X" and "here's what defenders would see."
2. **No secondary escalation path.** The lab as documented has exactly one path to Domain Admin. A senior reviewer will ask "what else did you find?" — a second path (e.g., an ACL abuse edge, unconstrained delegation, or an AS-REP roastable account) would demonstrate breadth, not just depth on one technique.
3. **No post-DA impact demonstration.** Once `svc_sql` was confirmed Domain Admin, a real assessment typically demonstrates *impact* (e.g., DCSync/NTDS.dit extraction) to make the finding concrete for a client, rather than stopping at privilege confirmation. This was correctly scoped out and listed under Future Improvements, which is the right call for a v1 — but it's the natural next addition.

## Consolidated Recommendations (Priority Order)

1. **Add the detection/telemetry layer.** Enable Sysmon + Security auditing on DC01, re-run the Kerberoasting step, and capture/annotate the actual Event ID 4769 log entry. This is the single highest-leverage addition — it turns this from an "attacker" project into an "attacker + defender" project, which is what most internal AD/blue-adjacent roles actually want to see.
2. **Add a second privilege-escalation path.** Even something small — an AS-REP Roastable account (BloodHound already has the query available and unused in this lab) or a GPO/ACL abuse edge — shows you can find more than one thing.
3. **Demonstrate post-compromise impact** (DCSync or NTDS.dit extraction against your own lab DC) to close the loop from "Domain Admin confirmed" to "here's what that actually lets you do to the business."
4. **Redact before publishing.** Follow the redaction list in `notes/screenshot-mapping.md` — blur both plaintext passwords and the AD Description-field password hint before this goes on a public GitHub profile.
5. **Consider a short (60–90 second) narrated video walkthrough** of the BloodHound Kerberoastable-accounts query through to the WinRM shell. Static screenshots are good; a recruiter watching you drive BloodHound live for 60 seconds is a stronger signal of authentic hands-on skill than any writeup.

**Bottom line:** this is a genuinely strong portfolio piece as-is — well above the typical "ran three tools in a CTF" submission — and the five items above are refinements that would take it from "strong junior/mid-level portfolio project" to "this person could walk into a mid-level internal pentest role and be productive on day one."
